What the shape says

Conviction against outcome

Colourways per style is the closest public read on how much conviction the plan put behind a style: a style bought in four colours was expected to sell harder than one bought in two. Discount rate is the closest read on whether that landed. Where the two disagree, there is a planning question worth asking.

Conviction index is colourways per style indexed to the average across all tiers and markets; above 100 means the plan committed more depth than average. Percentages marked † come from fewer than 20 options and move too much to build a decision on, so they are left unflagged.

What I would do about it

Three actions, each tied to a number above and each with the thing I would watch to know whether it worked.

    Method, and what this cannot tell you

    Every number here comes from public product listings collected by hand on one day. That gives an honest read on what is offered and what is currently reduced. It cannot tell you units sold, margin, stock cover, or intake plan, so nothing here is a claim about H&M's actual performance.

    Two proxies do the analytical work. Colourways per style stands in for planned conviction. Share of options currently discounted stands in for where that conviction did not convert. Both are directional. With real selling data — sell-through by week, stock cover, contribution after markdown — the same framework would produce a firmer answer, and the recommendations would be sized rather than directional.

    A one-day snapshot also catches each market at a different point in its markdown cycle. Repeating the collection weekly across a season would separate structural differences from timing differences, which is the version of this I would run inside the business.
